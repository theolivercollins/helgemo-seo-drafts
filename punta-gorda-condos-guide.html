A Guide to Condos in Punta Gorda

By Oliver HelgemoBuyingNovember 10, 2026

Punta Gorda & Charlotte County, FL

Quick Answer

Buy the building as carefully as you buy the unit.

Punta Gorda condos range from downtown mid-rises to villas near the water, and the building matters as much as the view. Since Florida's 2022 condominium reforms, buyers should ask for the milestone inspection report, the Structural Integrity Reserve Study, the budget, and any special assessments before an offer.

If you are searching for condos for sale in Punta Gorda, sort them by building type and age first, then compare units.

What Punta Gorda condos actually include

A Punta Gorda condominium search can return a downtown mid-rise, a low-rise building near a canal, a villa in a marina community, or a townhome-style unit. They are all condominiums or similar shared-ownership homes, but they behave very differently when you buy, insure, and sell them. The useful first questions are how many stories the building has, how old it is, and what the association is responsible for.

The city's condominiums are concentrated downtown near the harbor and in pockets of Punta Gorda Isles and Burnt Store Isles. The Historic District puts you closest to the Harborwalk and downtown dining. Burnt Store Marina, which sits just across the Lee County line with a Punta Gorda ZIP code, is the largest concentration of villas and multi-unit homes with boat slips. Our Punta Gorda real estate guide explains how those areas fit together.

The building first: milestone inspections and reserve studies

Florida changed the rules for condominium buildings after the 2021 Surfside collapse. The statute now requires a milestone inspection for buildings three habitable stories or taller, performed by December 31 of the year the building reaches 30 years of age, then every 10 years. If a local agency finds that local conditions require it, including environmental conditions such as proximity to salt water, the inspection is due at 25 years. Phase one is a visual examination by a licensed architect or engineer, and phase two, which is more detailed, is required only if substantial structural deterioration is found.

The second requirement is the Structural Integrity Reserve Study, usually called a SIRS. It is a reserve study for the structural parts of the building, and it must cover roof, structural systems, fireproofing and fire protection, plumbing, electrical systems, waterproofing and exterior painting, windows and exterior doors, and any other element whose replacement exceeds a set dollar threshold that the state updates each year. Associations in existence on July 1, 2022 had to finish the first one by December 31, 2025, and the state now has associations report completion through an online account. It is required at least every 10 years for buildings of three or more habitable stories.

None of that decides whether one building is a good purchase, and the law applies only to buildings of three stories or more. What it gives a buyer is a document trail. When you are looking at a condominium, ask for the inspection report if one applies, the SIRS, the budget, the reserve balances, and any proposed assessments. Have your attorney or inspector read them. A building that has done the work and funded the reserves is a different purchase from one that has not.

Downtown, the Isles, or the marina

Downtown and the Historic District. This is the walkable option, close to the harbor, parks, shops, and restaurants. Buyers here usually trade garden space for walkability, and building age and story count vary, so the milestone and SIRS questions matter a lot. See our guide to things to do in Punta Gorda for what is nearby.

Punta Gorda Isles and Burnt Store Isles. In the canal neighborhoods, condominiums and attached villas sit among single-family homes. Canal assessments and seawall responsibility may be part of ownership, so ask who owns the seawall and what the association or the city assessment covers. The City of Punta Gorda funds seawall and canal work in Punta Gorda Isles and Burnt Store Isles through non-ad valorem canal maintenance assessments.

Burnt Store Marina. The marina community combines villas, multi-unit buildings, a marina, and a golf course on Charlotte Harbor. Our Burnt Store Marina guide covers the community, the county line, and the slip question in detail. If a slip matters to you, treat it as a separate purchase with its own terms.

Buying a condo in Punta Gorda: a few local realities

Insurance is a building question and a unit question. The association typically carries a master policy on the building, and you carry a separate policy on your unit and belongings. Ask what the master policy covers, what the deductible is, and how storm and flood coverage are handled. Costs change and are specific to the building, so this guide will not invent a premium.

Read the documents, not the listing. Rental rules, pet rules, age or occupancy restrictions, and special-assessment history live in the declaration, the bylaws, and the budget, and they can change which buyers will want your unit when you sell.

Homestead. If the unit will be your primary residence, ask the Charlotte County Property Appraiser about Florida's homestead exemption, which is up to $50,000 of assessed value with the first $25,000 applying to all property taxes. You must own it and live there on January 1 and apply by March 1.

Storm history. Hurricane Ian made a second landfall near Punta Gorda on September 28, 2022, according to the National Hurricane Center's final report, and Charley came ashore in the same area in 2004. Ask each association about its storm history and its repairs.

The FHFA house-price index for the Punta Gorda metro area was 391.34 in the second quarter of 2026, down 0.2 percent from a year earlier and 7.7 percent below the second quarter of 2024. It is a repeat-transaction index for the whole metro area, not a condominium price. Ask for recent comparable sales within the same building or a similar one. Frequently asked questions

What should I ask before buying a condo in Punta Gorda?

Ask for the milestone inspection report if one applies, the Structural Integrity Reserve Study, the current budget, reserve balances, any proposed or approved special assessments, and the association's rental and pet rules. Have your attorney or inspector review them.

What is a milestone inspection?

A structural inspection required for condominium and cooperative buildings three habitable stories or taller. Under Florida Statute 553.899, it is due by December 31 of the year the building reaches 30 years of age, or 25 years where the local enforcement agency determines local conditions require it, and then every 10 years.

What is a Structural Integrity Reserve Study?

A SIRS is a reserve study of the structural parts of a condominium building, such as roof, structure, fire protection, plumbing, electrical systems, waterproofing, and windows. It is required at least every 10 years for buildings three habitable stories or taller.

Where are condos concentrated in Punta Gorda?

Mainly downtown near the harbor, in parts of Punta Gorda Isles and Burnt Store Isles, and in marina communities such as Burnt Store Marina. The census data show that 22.3 percent of the city's housing units are in buildings with five or more units.

Is Burnt Store Marina part of the city of Punta Gorda?

No. Burnt Store Marina is an unincorporated community in Lee County, just across the county line, with a Punta Gorda ZIP code (33955). Verify jurisdiction and services for any specific address.

Does homestead exemption apply to condos?

It can, if the unit is your permanent residence on January 1 and you apply with the county property appraiser by March 1. Only the property appraiser determines eligibility.

Are condos cheaper than houses in Punta Gorda?

Not necessarily. The public data we use do not give a condominium-only median, and prices vary by building, age, view, and water access. Ask for recent comparable sales for the specific building or one like it.
