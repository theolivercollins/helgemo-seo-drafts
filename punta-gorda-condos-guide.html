A Guide to Condos in Punta Gorda

By Brian HelgemoBuyingNovember 10, 2026

Punta Gorda & Charlotte County, FL

Buy the building as carefully as you buy the unit. In Punta Gorda, that sentence matters more than the view from the balcony.

A Punta Gorda condominium search can return a downtown mid-rise, a low-rise near a canal, a villa in a marina community, or a townhome-style unit. They all share ownership of something, and they behave very differently when you buy, insure, and sell them.

Since Florida's condominium reforms after the 2021 Surfside collapse, buyers also get something they did not used to: a paper trail. This guide shows you which documents to ask for, in what order, and what they tell you.

Quick Answer

Ask for the milestone report, the SIRS, the budget, and any special assessments before an offer.

Florida now requires milestone inspections and Structural Integrity Reserve Studies for condominium buildings three habitable stories or taller. A building that has done the work and funded the reserves is a different purchase from one that has not.

Part 02 · The rules

What Florida requires, in plain English

Two requirements, both for buildings three habitable stories or taller.

Milestone inspection

Required by December 31 of the year the building reaches 30 years of age, then every 10 years. If the local enforcement agency finds local conditions require it, such as proximity to salt water, the first one is due at 25 years. Phase one is a visual examination by a licensed architect or engineer. Phase two, which is more detailed, is required only if substantial structural deterioration is found.

SIRS

A Structural Integrity Reserve Study, required at least every 10 years. It covers roof, structural systems, fireproofing and fire protection, plumbing, electrical systems, waterproofing and exterior painting, windows and exterior doors, and any other element whose replacement exceeds a dollar threshold the state updates each year.

Deadline

Associations existing on July 1, 2022 had to finish the first SIRS by December 31, 2025. A later deadline of December 31, 2026 applies where the study is done together with a milestone inspection due by then. The state has associations report completion through an online account.

Source: Florida Statutes, section 553.899; Florida DBPR, condominium inspections and SIRS.

What the law does not do

None of that decides whether one building is a good purchase, and the law applies only to buildings of three stories or more. What it gives a buyer is a document trail. Have your attorney or inspector read it. This post is general information, not legal advice, and statutory deadlines can change, so confirm current requirements with your attorney.

Part 03 · The process

The five-step building review

Do these in order, before you write an offer.

01

Find out how tall and how old the building is

Story count and age tell you whether a milestone inspection and SIRS apply at all, and how soon the next one is due.

02

Request the inspection report and the SIRS

If they apply, ask for both. Have your attorney or inspector read them, not just the cover page.

03

Read the budget and the reserve balances

A building that has funded its reserves is in a different position from one that has not.

04

Ask about special assessments, approved or proposed

These are the line items that change what you actually pay. Get them in writing.

05

Read the declaration, bylaws, and rules

Rental rules, pet rules, age or occupancy restrictions, and assessment history live here, not in the listing. They can also change which buyers will want your unit when you sell.

Part 05 · Reality check

Buying a condo here: a few local realities

Insurance, storms, and the tax paperwork.

01

Insurance is a building question and a unit question

The association typically carries a master policy on the building, and you carry a separate policy on your unit and belongings. Ask what the master policy covers, what the deductible is, and how storm and flood coverage are handled. Costs are specific to the building, so this guide will not invent a premium.

02

Storm history and homestead

Hurricane Ian made a second landfall near Punta Gorda on September 28, 2022, according to the National Hurricane Center, and Charley came ashore in the same area in 2004. Ask each association about its storm history and its repairs. For homestead, ask the Charlotte County Property Appraiser about Florida's homestead exemption, which is up to $50,000 of assessed value with the first $25,000 applying to all property taxes. You must own and live in the home as your permanent residence on January 1 and apply by March 1.

Part 06

Condo questions, answered

Q1

What should I ask before buying a condo in Punta Gorda?

Ask for the milestone inspection report if one applies, the Structural Integrity Reserve Study, the current budget, reserve balances, any proposed or approved special assessments, and the association's rental and pet rules. Have your attorney or inspector review them.

Q2

What is a milestone inspection?

A structural inspection required for condominium and cooperative buildings three habitable stories or taller. Under Florida Statute 553.899, it is due by December 31 of the year the building reaches 30 years of age, or 25 years where the local enforcement agency determines local conditions require it, and then every 10 years.

Q3

What is a Structural Integrity Reserve Study?

A SIRS is a reserve study of the structural parts of a condominium building, such as roof, structure, fire protection, plumbing, electrical systems, waterproofing, and windows. It is required at least every 10 years for buildings three habitable stories or taller.

Q4

Where are condos concentrated in Punta Gorda?

Mainly downtown near the harbor, in parts of Punta Gorda Isles and Burnt Store Isles, and in marina communities such as Burnt Store Marina. The census data show that 22.3 percent of the city's housing units are in buildings with five or more units.

Q5

Is Burnt Store Marina part of the city of Punta Gorda?

No. Burnt Store Marina is an unincorporated community in Lee County, just across the county line, with a Punta Gorda ZIP code (33955). Verify jurisdiction and services for any specific address.

Q6

Does homestead exemption apply to condos?

It can, if the unit is your permanent residence on January 1 and you apply with the county property appraiser by March 1. Only the property appraiser determines eligibility.

Q7

Are condos cheaper than houses in Punta Gorda?

Not necessarily. The public data we use do not give a condominium-only median, and prices vary by building, age, view, and water access. Ask for recent comparable sales for the specific building or one like it.
